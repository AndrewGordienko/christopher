# Andrew Outbound Voice

For an unanswered same-recipient paid-contract thread, route to [paid-contract-followup-editor](../paid-contract-followup-editor/SKILL.md). Its September 17 user-selected Gmail examples control the writing: firsthand work context leads to personal concern about unequal access, a concrete recipient-specific benefit and skill connection, then a conditional 4–6 week paid contract. Preserve the roughly 300-word progression and ordinary language; do not flatten it into a bump, force a backlog CTA, or restore the removed CEO-proximity sentence. Do not apply this full follow-up structure to other message types.

Use the [Andrew email workflow](../andrew-email-style/SKILL.md) for passive Sent/thread retrieval, current factual context, relationship, persistence and final checks. This is the writer pass, not prospect qualification. For Morrow/Wapahki pilots require the qualified-cell packet; for paid Contract Work use its distinct targeting and current contract guidance.

Sound like Andrew: technically curious, understated, intelligent but conversational, slightly informal, ordinary English with natural sentence-length variation. The requested young-founder tone is not permission to insert an unverified age or biography.

Let one thought cause the next: work → observed system → plausible issue → overlap with the work → smallest useful test → answerable ask. This is a reasoning chain, not six mandatory paragraphs. Preserve a strong supplied draft; do not force prose into slots or a word count. Use current instructions, then the task draft, same-person history, similar Sent examples and broader voice evidence.

Avoid default “caught my attention”, “I came across”, “A useful pilot could”, “This would provide”, “meaningful productive time”, “material issue”, “operational burden”, “unlock”, “leverage”, “optimize”, “solution”, “value proposition”, “I'd love to”, “exciting”, generic compliments and em dashes. Do not manufacture enthusiasm, motivation or variation. Use fewer repeated “I” openings where the thought naturally permits it.

Ordinary constructions can help: “That made me wonder…”, “If operators are having to…”, “The interesting part for us is…”, “We're trying to find one…”, “If that's actually happening on the line…”. These are examples, not replacements to stamp across a batch.

Subjects are short, boring and concrete: “Sorting robot recovery” or “Granby sorting robots”. A subject like “Robot stops at Arrow Road” requires actual evidence of stops; otherwise ask without asserting the fault. Keep greetings/sign-offs natural to Andrew's actual mail. Broad spam-word lists do not override his explicitly chosen wording or ordinary truthful technical language.

Save one selected subject/body plus its source/qualification fingerprint. Do not declare the writer's own draft approved; the two separate critic passes follow.

For Contract Work, the latest [human-progression direction](../andrew-email-style/references/contract-useful-work.md) takes precedence: use Neil/FIDO and the supplied Archy/Nic drafts as seeds. Preserve curiosity and genuine motivation while making the small contract ask clear. Do not turn the email into credentials → deliverable → paid contract → backlog → 15 minutes. The recent transactional versions are rejected alternatives. Keep useful specificity, uncertainty and deference without enforcing particular CTA words.
