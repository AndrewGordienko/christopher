# OutageHub: existing workflow to embedded customer

Work in Andrew's outreach repository at `/Users/andrewgordienko/Documents/christopher`. Read [Andrew's email workflow](../andrew-email-style/SKILL.md) for private history, evidence capture, separate research/writing passes, and local queue operations. Sending remains a separate action. Sender: `andrew.g@outagehub.ca`.

Find companies that already operate a product, customer workflow or operational process into which Canadian outage information can be inserted with a specific benefit and little product change. Prefer software platforms distributing the capability to many customers. A large site owner is a secondary target. Do not sell map advertising, sponsorships or custom utility software projects in this motion.

## Product truth before the pitch

Use the live Canadian outage API as the entry product, location-based monitoring/notifications as the main commercial opportunity, and historical data as a secondary data sale. The public map is proof and a demo. Verify which products are available now versus proposed; keep coverage, refresh interval, geographic matching, historical depth, licensing, price and support unknown when unsupported. Deck estimates are hypotheses, not a price list. Utility-reported geographic overlap is context, not proof that an individual building lost power, a fault diagnosis, or a restoration guarantee.

## Find the insertion point

Prioritize critical-event/emergency notification products; telecom/NOC monitoring; multi-site facility operations; catastrophe/property-risk products; infrastructure monitoring; weather/geospatial platforms; municipal response software; and EV charging/fleet systems.

Require a named existing product and primary evidence of its workflow: site/asset monitoring, external event overlays, location rules, notifications, dashboards/APIs, or risk/reliability analytics. The minimum explanation is: **Today they do X. With OutageHub they could additionally do Y.** If X is unsupported or Y is only generic interest in outages, leave the account unqualified.

For each account record in `campaigns/<slug>/accounts/<account>/`:

- Company, verified domain, parent/pause group and relevant product.
- Existing workflow and dated sources with exact supporting excerpts.
- Proposed insertion point, API/Notifications/Historical fit, concrete beneficiary and conditional benefit.
- Canadian relevance: distinguish Canadian customers/sites from headquarters or global availability.
- Existing outage capability and suppliers. Absence from a product page is **unknown**, not proof of a gap. Refresh the [competitive baseline](../andrew-email-style/references/outagehub-competitive-baseline.md).
- Why buy versus maintain utility integrations internally, plus why evaluate OutageHub versus another feed. These are hypotheses until the buyer confirms. Do not claim Canada exclusivity or superior coverage/latency/price.
- Likely product/data/engineering/operations/partnership owner. Find 3–5 real Apollo contacts where available, rank the best three for the existing writer/queue interface, and retain additional researched people as alternates. Never complete obfuscated names or guess addresses. Enrich only ranked people at qualified accounts; no personal emails or phone enrichment.
- Seven sourced ordinal scores from 0–5: integration fit, existing relevant workflow, economic pain, Canadian exposure, ability to buy, ease of integration, distribution leverage. Unknown remains null. Show raw total /35, evidence coverage and a separately weighted priority; distribution has the largest weight. Scores are research judgments, not probabilities or verified budgets.
- One outreach angle and the next unanswered qualification question.

Workflow qualification permits a **discovery conversation** when product/workflow evidence is strong and the prospect's current outage supplier is unknown. Record this explicitly with `qualification_scope: workflow_discovery`; never mark the data gap verified or advance to evaluation/integration/customer from public research. Known equivalent coverage requires a specific supported incremental use case or hold the lead.

## Learn from the first 100

Screen 100 distinct companies before expanding further unless Andrew specifies another scope. Record qualified, held and rejected accounts and reasons; a target number never justifies fabricated matches. Deduplicate brands under the same buying organization. Inspect the best 25 in depth, then prepare the requested email batch. Normally use one first contact per account per day, with alternates available for later account-level decisions.

Cluster by repeated **workflow and integration point**, not industry labels alone. Report counts and evidence strength: e.g. event-to-location-to-alert, offline-site triage, charger incident attribution, building alarm context, portfolio risk enrichment. Distinguish a researched pattern from buyer demand. Pick a first vertical from repeated fit, then use substantive replies, evaluations and paid integrations to confirm or revise it. Stop broad prospecting when one vertical earns a clear case for focused work.
