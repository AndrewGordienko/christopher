# Cold Email Skeptic

For unanswered same-person paid-contract follow-ups, use the [current follow-up mode and Andrew-selected Gmail examples](../paid-contract-followup-editor/SKILL.md). Read the original thread before applying the questions below. Its roughly 300-word work-to-motivation progression, shared factual context, named teams, Sierra Leone comparison and explicit 4–6 week paid ask are deliberate user preferences. The original thread already establishes the company context, so do not reject this mode merely because the new company paragraph comes third. Check its concrete relevance, truthful claims and conditional project ask; do not force a short bump, question mark, backlog CTA or promised deliverable. Scheduled examples establish voice, not conversion performance.

Start with the first eight seconds of reading as a busy, mildly skeptical recipient, then check the full email. Answer with concrete reasons:

1. Why did Andrew contact this recipient specifically?
2. What issue does he think may exist?
3. Is the proposed mechanism technically plausible?
4. Is the ask small?
5. Does he sound like a real technical founder?
6. Can someone reply usefully even if they are the wrong person?

Reject generic plant personalization, an unidentified cell in a Morrow pilot, unnecessarily broad scope, unclear product purpose, abstract benefit, an asserted unverified failure, a commitment-heavy CTA, or a specific reason that arrives too late. Shared introductions and ordinary asks can repeat; do not paraphrase good copy merely for diversity.

Return PASS, EDIT or RESEARCH MORE. Missing evidence means RESEARCH MORE, not WRITE MORE GENERICALLY. For paid Contract Work, apply the same reader test to its defined technical project without imposing the robotic-cell gate on that separate mode.

Record all six answers, final draft hash, applicable qualification hash, reviewer, time and reason under `cold_email_skeptic` in `outbound-reviews.json`. Any later subject/body/qualification change invalidates this pass. Follow with existing grammar, fact, voice, readability and enabled Morrow editorial checks on exact final copy. Only then may the email enter review; none of these passes authorizes sending.

Apply the latest [Contract human-progression preference](../andrew-email-style/references/contract-useful-work.md): “small contract project” can make the ask clear without the word “paid.” Do not require a backlog question, timed call or deliverable checklist. Reject a generic credential-to-CTA pitch that deletes the specific interest, skill connection and genuine motivation. Also reject an implementation promise that assumes unconfirmed access. Preserve a strong supplied draft rather than replacing it with a mechanically commercial version.
