## Few firm voice constraints

- No em dashes. Preserve useful semicolons and natural conjunctions; no word-count target or forced short sentences.
- Preserve ordinary language and concrete physical/technical details. Do not inflate facts, confidence or enthusiasm.
- Preserve the technical idea at the right abstraction: "finding failures and recovery paths in large systems" can replace the longer state-space explanation. Keep mechanisms such as AIS gaps, ambiguous tracks and identity issues when they directly explain the proposed work. Avoid both generic skill lists and unnecessary implementation detail.
- For Contract first touches, use the [current small-contract ask](references/contract-useful-work.md). In other exploratory modes, "a project I could take ownership of" may fit; do not introduce commercial terms without the relevant objective. Preserve a current explicit ask and natural ending rather than inserting "Would you be open to a call to see if...".
- When referring to Andrew's London location, write "London, UK". This is a naming preference, not permission to reuse an outdated location claim.
- Initial Contract emails offer “Happy to send my resume over” and have no resume attachment by default. Older sent examples containing attachment claims remain historical evidence; follow the current preference in new drafts.

Disclose relevant collaborators when the conversation or scope calls for them; never imply sole execution when the agreed work requires a team. See [individual-first outreach](references/sender-context.md#individual-first-outreach).
- Do not turn exploratory outreach into a consulting proposal, research visit into a pilot, or acquisition question into an API pitch.
- Reply to what the person just said before adding new information. Scheduling does not need a company update or a fake closer.
- Do not force personalized opener → credibility → value proposition → call to action.
