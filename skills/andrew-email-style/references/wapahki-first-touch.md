# Wapahki first touch: a concrete offer, remote first

## Current cell gate (September 2026)

For new Morrow/Wapahki pilot outreach, [morrow-pilot-qualifier](../../morrow-pilot-qualifier/SKILL.md) and the other five linked stages in the main skill supersede older automation-only selection, plant-wide baseline offers and volume targets below. A scored QUALIFIED cell is required before writing; POSSIBLE means more research, HOLD means no outreach. Historic examples remain history. First scope: one cell, passive faults/actions/restored-state observation, repeatable human intervention time, then one separately authorized suitable recovery. Neither a robot installation nor downstream manual sorting proves recurring operator recovery. The UofT student identity is supported; a formal university research-project affiliation requires separate evidence.

Andrew's latest direction, 15 September 2026, supersedes the earlier conversation/visit and research-question framing, including the older Suresh and Steeve examples. Rewrite the existing batch when asked; changing this reference alone does not repair queued copy. Preserve sending approval requirements and invalidate approvals when copy changes.

The first-touch objective is to find out whether an intervention problem is material enough to justify a short remote technical call and a scoped pilot. The commercial destination is a [qualified signed pilot LOI](wapahki-loi.md), not a cold LOI ask. A plant receives an operational benefit; it is not being asked to educate Andrew or provide training data as a favour.

Default introduction:

“I’m a UofT student building Wapahki, software for reducing the time automated systems lose when someone has to step in and recover them.”

Connect that thought to one sourced facility process, the operational loss that may exist, and what a pilot would do for the plant. Personalize the business case, not just a question about its machinery. Finish by asking whether the problem is real enough for a short call, usually about 20 minutes. Andrew is currently in London, UK: no “could I visit”, “coming by”, “I'd like to learn” or general plant-tour CTA. A later video walkthrough, remote technical scoping or Canadian-side visit may be useful; a site visit is not required to reach an LOI.

Internally establish process → plausible intervention → productive time/process impact → customer metric → baseline and recovery evaluation. Research actual automation and candidate data sources before drafting. Never invent intervention frequency, available logs, downtime, losses, KPI ownership, recoverability or savings. When these are unknown, offer to identify which interventions cost the most productive time and establish the baseline. Candidate guardrails are proposed, not confirmed management KPIs.

The pilot offer is to use relevant existing records, when available and agreed, to baseline interventions, rank recurring recovery cases, build/evaluate recovery software and compare results against output/quality/safety guardrails. Mentioning data as the mechanism is appropriate; requesting that the recipient send logs or grant access cold is a different stage. No cold control request, formal pilot commitment or LOI ask. Do not promise automatic recovery or access rights. Later assisted/automatic recovery applies only to separately scoped, technically appropriate and approved cases.

Adapt to the actual recipient:

- Technical/maintenance: available alarms and machine state, intervention sequences and repeatable recovery cases; explain their use only when helpful.
- Operations: productive time, intervention burden, output and relevant quality/yield guardrails.
- Executive/corporate engineering: a measurable result at one named process, then whether the approach transfers across facilities. Do not claim proven generality.
- Routing or unconfirmed site remit: retain the concrete offer but ask whether they cover the named site or can direct it to its owner. A title or email does not prove facility authority.

Plain subjects name the process and automation/recovery topic. Remove “visit” and “recovery data”; use “robot” only with actual supported robotics evidence. Preserve a good subject rather than mechanically renaming every email.

The plant contributes technical context, a technical owner and relevant existing data when mutually agreed. Wapahki contributes the intervention baseline, failure analysis, prioritization, recovery evaluation/software and measured before/after result. Site data and cross-deployment/model-improvement rights remain separately negotiated. Never frame this as “give us data to train our model.”

Review all facility paragraphs and CTAs together. The explicit shared introduction and ordinary call asks may repeat. Reject copied data-architecture paragraphs, noun-swapped offers without a process-specific business case, known-failure assertions from hypotheses and visit/research-interview framing. Run the campaign lint plus a separate semantic review; review exact final facts, grammar and voice after any edits.
