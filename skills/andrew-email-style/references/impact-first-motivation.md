# Do not manufacture motivation

Use impact to select worthwhile organizations, not as a compulsory sentence in every email. Andrew’s broad goal of useful work does not supply a distinctive personal motivation for each company.

The societal value can be implicit in the problem, but do not require a direct jump from biography to company fact. [Preserve the original thought](contract-preserve-thought.md): genuine interest and a natural explanation of how Andrew arrived at this company may be the strongest part of a draft.

Inspect broad prefaces such as “I’ve been looking for ways to use my skills to…” or “problems that improve people’s lives” for invented or interchangeable motivation. They are diagnostic cues, not forbidden constructions. Keep a genuine thought that reads naturally; do not remove it or synonym-swap merely to satisfy a phrase rule.

Rejected:
> I’ve been looking for ways to use that skillset on problems that actually improve people’s lives, and Aclima’s mobile mapping work interested me because…

Preferred:
> With Aclima's mobile mapping, I'd be curious how much the route changes what you learn about where pollution is worst.

Rejected:
> I’ve been looking for planning problems where a better decision means less wasted fuel, which is what drew me to OpenAirlines.

Preferred:
> The planning work has made me interested in what happens when a recommendation no longer holds. With OpenAirlines, I'd be curious when a changed weather forecast is enough to make the original recommendation worth revising.

Preferred:
> I was interested in the fire-spread modelling because uncertainty in the starting perimeter or wind forecast can carry through the rest of the simulation.

Preserve genuinely distinctive motivation Andrew actually supplied, such as wanting to work on ocean conservation in the Global Fishing Watch draft. Do not infer personal beliefs from a sector or suppress every natural expression of interest.

## Concrete-problem test

Consider the email without the motivation sentence. Remove it only if it is actually manufactured or adds nothing and the email keeps its natural continuity. If removal makes the email flatter or removes the reason Andrew reached this company, preserve the thought and repair only the defective wording. Do not insert a moral bridge merely to connect points.

For a motivation-only correction, preserve the subject, biography, technical project idea, ask and ending. Remove or minimally reconnect only the failing construction. Do not turn this into another general rewrite.

## Research and subjects

Keep societal_outcome, technical_problem, and andrew_connection in the research packet, with sourced facts separated from hypotheses. Impact guides selection; it need not be narrated in the email. Current research supports the company’s work, not Andrew’s feelings.

Use concrete topic subjects. Avoid self-focused “UofT student hoping”, “Using my AI skills”, or “Could my skills”. Neither every subject nor every project idea needs the same grammar.

## Lessons from the first sent batch

Gecko and GoodMaps: remove broad safety/accessibility motivation when the inspection/navigation question already explains relevance. Floodbase: the actual missing-observation versus changing-flood question is stronger than a preface about applying simulation somewhere. Enthought: omit defensive qualifications about not bringing a project that does not fit; simply ask about the research software component. Avoid restating the same uncertainty list in both interest and project paragraphs. These are forward-looking lessons, never edits to sent history.
