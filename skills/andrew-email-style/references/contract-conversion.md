# Contract work: reply to delivery

Optimize for a useful bounded project Andrew can own, not a volume of replies or consulting proposals. Discovery and first-touch copy are only the beginning. Resume the account's persisted record for every reply, meeting, scope, agreement, payment and delivery task.

## Reply and next action

Read the complete current thread. Classify the actual response separately from deciding what to do. Answer the person's question first, cancel invalid no-response sends, pause other cold contacts at the account, and persist the evidence and next action.

Interest does not mean a meeting is confirmed or a project exists. If someone agrees to talk, the next action is to arrange or confirm the conversation. If someone asks for a scope, preparing that scope outranks another cold email. Follow [CRM and sequencing](crm-and-sequencing.md) and preserve unknowns.

## Before a technical call

Prepare one page from current account research, correspondence and agreed meeting details:

- What the organization does and what this person owns, with sources.
- Andrew's original project hypothesis, explicitly distinguished from anything the recipient has confirmed.
- What the recipient actually said, relevant constraints and unresolved questions.
- One or two plausible directions, without presenting an agreed scope.
- What Andrew needs to learn to decide whether he can finish something useful.
- Five to eight concrete questions suited to this conversation.

Useful questions concern the work itself: what needs doing that nobody has time to own, how it is handled now, what has already been tried, what a useful result would change, what data/code is available, what could block completion, who would review the work and who can authorize a project. Do not mechanically ask all eight or turn the call into a qualification checklist.

A tentative date is not a calendar booking. Link actual confirmation/calendar evidence before recording a meeting as confirmed. Never invent a transcript or attendance.

## After the call

Ingest Andrew's notes or the actual transcript. Extract the problem, examples, constraints, data/dependencies, success criteria, commitments, owners and dates, with quotations/timestamps or note references. Distinguish explicit commitments, suggestions and Andrew's interpretation.

Record what changed from the original hypothesis. The useful project discovered on the call outranks the cold-email idea. Draft the follow-up around their conversation, not the initial pitch. Set a dated next action for each commitment. Ambiguous attribution stays unresolved instead of becoming a CRM fact.

## Define the project

When there is a real problem, prepare a compact brief:

1. Problem and why solving this piece would be useful.
2. Goal and observable result.
3. Proposed work and explicit exclusions where needed.
4. Deliverables and how the recipient will assess them.
5. Timeline, start conditions and review points.
6. Client-provided data, access and technical point of contact.
7. Open dependencies and uncertainties that could change scope.
8. Proposed fee/currency and payment schedule, when Andrew supplies or authorizes them.

Choose a piece Andrew can realistically finish. Do not compress unknown research into a guaranteed result or invent a one-to-three-week timeline merely to fit the campaign goal. If the uncertainty itself is the work, make the investigation and its output concrete.

Use relevant current facts to propose scope. Keep inferred scope, estimated price, quoted price, accepted price and contracted price distinct. Never send a quote or agree to terms simply because a template contains a number. A pricing recommendation should explain its scope assumptions; missing facts stay marked, not silently filled.

## Agreement, payment and delivery

Use the existing opportunity/project-agreement helpers for a one-page draft once the actual problem, work, deliverables, timeline, dependencies, fee and currency are known. Keep missing terms visible for review. The generated document is not legal approval, an agreement sent, a signature or permission to begin work.

Persist agreement versions and actual send/signature evidence. Track deposit requested, promised, charged and settled separately. A paid invoice supports a payment event; record bank settlement separately before calling it available cash. A funded escrow is neither a released payment nor a bank receipt. Do not mark cash collected from an email promising payment. Andrew's current commercial instructions govern whether work begins before signature/payment.

After signing, maintain delivery milestones, dependencies, client review, acceptance and remaining payment as dated tasks. A deliverable being uploaded is not necessarily accepted. After completion, consider a useful follow-up, referral or next project based on the real relationship, with no automatic message.

## Stalled opportunities

Check active opportunities daily. Surface the actual missing step: interest without scheduling, a promised scope not prepared, an unanswered agreement, a dependency not provided or overdue client review. Show the relevant event/date, expected next action and owner.

Use explicit commitments/deadlines first. Configurable inactivity windows can flag a review when none exists, but do not fabricate urgency or send a generic bump. Choose reply, clarify, wait, close or follow up based on current evidence. Every active opportunity needs a dated next action or a deliberate visible hold.

## Learn from the full path

Track selected account → selected recipient → useful reply → call → actual project → quoted/accepted price → signed agreement → received payment → delivered result. Keep each denominator and attribution explicit. Evaluate recipient/problem categories on project progression as well as replies; no causal conclusions from a small sample.

Use existing sourced research, contacts, thread state, tasks and opportunities rather than creating another disconnected CRM. Keep capabilities honest: this playbook does not mean calendar ingestion, transcript capture, payment reconciliation or autonomous close execution exists. Inspect the actual integration and persist its evidence before claiming completion.
