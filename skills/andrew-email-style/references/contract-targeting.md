# Targeting buyers of bounded technical work

Use this before Contract Work discovery and ranking, alongside the current mission and [conversion workflow](contract-conversion.md). Select a useful problem with a credible purchasing path. An interesting organization is not automatically a likely customer.

## Buying evidence

Keep these separate in the research packet:

- **Technical fit:** the actual product/problem and the part Andrew could move, supported by company work, code, papers or a full job description.
- **External-work evidence:** a relevant current contract role, identified research contractor, paid external research program, or evidence of an earlier comparable external project. Record function, date and source.
- **Budget and authority:** a person who owns the problem, a plausible financial approver, and what is actually known about available project funding.
- **Start friction:** whether a bounded piece can begin with accessible software, a small approved data extract or an existing simulator, versus onboarding, restricted data, site access or live-system integration.
- **Proof match:** one actual Andrew result that demonstrates the relevant capability, with a source and a shareable artifact if one has been verified.

Funding, growth and full-time hiring are supporting signals, not confirmation of contractor budget. A recruiting contract does not demonstrate a technical team's use of contractors. A firm selling consulting services is not evidence it buys subcontractors. Customer contracts, competition prizes and unpaid research collaborations are different buying paths. Do not mark them interchangeable.

Use honest buyer grades: **external-work precedent found**, **plausible buyer, unconfirmed**, or **poor near-term path**. Precedent is not a current commitment. Unknown budget, access or procurement stays unknown. Do not invent a probability of closing from headcount.

## Where and whom to investigate

### Current technical-owner preference

For Andrew's small individual contracts, research three routes in order: the person directly responsible for the proposed technical surface; a relevant manager or technical lead who could sponsor the work; an executive fallback only at a genuinely small company with evidence of hands-on responsibility. Prefer the first relevant, reachable owner. Do not fill all three slots with weak names or claim a reporting line that has not been established.

Engineering managers, autonomy/planning leads, ML or optimization leads, and specific research/simulation leads are strong starting points. A staff/principal engineer or scientist can be a champion without controlling budget. A relevant director can combine technical ownership with sponsorship. CEO, chairman, president, generic CTO and VP titles are not default first contacts. A lower title alone is insufficient: a camera-hardware engineer is not automatically the tracking-model owner, and a seed-manufacturing lead is not automatically the flight-planning owner.

Record both the technical route and the unresolved approval path. If the strongest replacement has no verified work address, hold that route rather than silently reverting to an executive to fill a send quota. Keep one active first contact per company; alternates are sequential options after history review. On retargeting, supersede the unsent executive draft, preserve its history, and rebind the draft checks to the replacement. Preserve the approved human progression and make only recipient-relevant copy changes.

Sector is a discovery dimension, not the deciding filter. Start with problems that match demonstrated capability: recovery/search, simulation and optimization, model evaluation/distillation, or scientific data tooling. Energy, transport, robotics and scientific software can all qualify; none is established as the highest-converting industry without Andrew's own results.

For a small technical startup, inspect the founder/CTO or engineering/research lead. In a larger team, identify the manager who owns the specific work and the approval path. At research organizations, investigate the PI, research director or program owner and applicable external-project funding. At software/engineering service firms, investigate a delivery lead only when there is evidence of capacity needs or subcontracting. Do not default to Recruiting or an individual scientist because they are easiest to find.

Prefer direct ownership over a mechanical title cutoff. A technical director or engineering manager may be a stronger route than a remote CEO; a hands-on technical founder or scientific executive can remain the right person. A staff engineer can champion work without authorizing its budget. Verify current employer/domain and responsibility independently: an acquisition can explain a parent-company address but does not prove the person still owns the subsidiary's product. Hold unresolved identity or affiliation conflicts and never guess a replacement address. Reuse an existing better-qualified contact instead of creating parallel cold approaches to the same account.

Prefer an offline, separable contribution when it materially lowers access and approval friction. Physical deployment is not required to make a robotics project useful. Conversely, do not pretend a simulation is useful when it cannot answer the team's actual question without unavailable data or models.

Before cold outreach, inspect known relationships, previous company contact and credible introductions. Previous contacts are excluded from a requested new first-touch wave; a warm re-engagement is a distinct task. Never fabricate an introduction or assume a shared university creates one.

## Proof and commercial progression

The resume establishes claimed experience; a demonstrable artifact makes it easier to assess. Select one relevant result, not the entire biography. Prefer verified public code, a reproducible experiment, a short demo or an authorized case note. Do not disclose employer code or imply measured business gains absent evidence. Preserve the fixed introduction unless Andrew asks to change it. Initial Contract emails offer the resume instead of attaching it.

After interest, discover the actual unfinished problem, acceptance criteria, available inputs, reviewer and approver. Then define a bounded piece and price it under Andrew's instructions. Track scope, agreement, deposit and delivery as distinct evidenced events. A warm reply is not a contract; an invoice is not cash received.

The repeatable business loop is relevant work → proof → right buyer → conversation → bounded paid project → delivered result → repeat work/referral. Email volume supplies opportunities to test this loop; it cannot replace any stage. Reserve capacity for delivery, calls and scoping instead of filling every available hour with outreach.

## Learn from outcomes

Compare buyer grade, problem category, proof used, sponsor role and acquisition path against useful replies, qualified calls, signed scope and cash received. Preserve denominators and uncertainty. Do not label a subject or industry a winner from same-day silence or a few replies.
