# Contract buyer targeting

Choose a problem Andrew can credibly deliver and a buyer with a plausible path to commissioning it. Do not infer willingness to buy from a company's industry, funding or technical sophistication.

Use the repository's [buyer research rules](../andrew-email-style/references/contract-targeting.md) and [reply-to-delivery workflow](../andrew-email-style/references/contract-conversion.md). Read the current campaign and account history before new discovery. Keep Morrow/Wapahki pilots on the separate [cell qualifier](../morrow-pilot-qualifier/SKILL.md).

## Research before choosing people

1. Identify the actual product, workflow and a bounded unfinished piece that matches one verified Andrew result.
2. Find dated evidence of external technical purchasing: a relevant contract opening, request for proposals, awarded work, supplier case study or named paid collaboration. Prefer the buyer's own material; label supplier claims. Record who bought from whom, the scope and event date.
3. Investigate the present need and timing separately. A historic purchase proves precedent, not current budget. A company receiving a customer contract is a supplier; subcontracting capacity is a hypothesis until supported. Grant awards and full-time vacancies do not prove external-project budget.
4. Find the person accountable for the actual work, a plausible sponsor/approver, and any necessary procurement or data owner. Verify current names, roles, employer/domain and responsibility. Hold unresolved identity or affiliation conflicts. Prefer the owner near the problem; retain a hands-on technical founder when relevant. Do not guess a replacement email. An engineer may be a useful champion without signing authority. Rank no more than three useful routes; do not default to executives or recruiting.
5. Assess what a first contribution requires: public code, simulator or an approved extract versus live deployment, private data and substantial onboarding. Do not assume access.
6. Check existing relationships and company replies. A warm conversation outranks a fresh cold contact. Do not turn an already-contacted person into a new first touch.

Use the current [technical-owner preference](../andrew-email-style/references/contract-targeting.md#current-technical-owner-preference): direct problem owner first, relevant manager or technical lead next, hands-on executive fallback only where justified at a small company. Research up to three useful routes without inventing reporting lines or filling slots with weak matches. An engineer can champion work without authorizing the fee. Hold unverified replacements rather than reverting to a CEO to fill a quota. Sector is a search direction, not a qualification verdict.

## Save a reviewable account record

Persist `campaigns/<slug>/accounts/<account>/buyer-research.json` and `buyer-research.md` with:

- technical problem and proposed bounded contribution, explicitly a hypothesis until the buyer confirms it;
- external-work precedent, buyer/supplier distinction and current trigger;
- named technical owner, sponsor and approval path, marking UNKNOWN where needed;
- available inputs/start friction and one sourced proof match;
- sources with URLs, observation/event dates and CONFIRMED / LIKELY / UNKNOWN claims;
- contact-history disposition, next research question and next action;
- buyer grade: **external-work precedent found**, **plausible buyer, unconfirmed**, or **poor near-term path**.

Keep evidence grade separate from outreach readiness. No contract probability or invented precision. A dated case-study example is not an evergreen target. Search the live web before recommending current accounts or people. Use available authorized history tools, reporting coverage limits honestly.

## Turn evidence into useful outreach

Finish research and strategy before invoking the writer. State one concrete reason to contact this owner, one relevant demonstration of capability, and a small answerable next step. Preserve Andrew's current introduction and voice instructions. Do not request broad data access, manufacture urgency, or propose a full consulting engagement on first touch.

Track qualified conversations, scoped work, agreements, cash received and delivered results by buyer grade/problem/role. Reply counts alone do not establish success. Small samples guide the next experiment; they do not establish a winning industry. Add volume only while maintaining qualification and capacity to follow through.
