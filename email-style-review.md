## What the skill carries forward

- Preserve your draft and sentence rhythm; keep contractions and natural acknowledgments.
- Keep ordinary replies short. Give first investor emails enough room to explain the technical problem and approach.
- Use concrete progress and practical language, such as something shifting, jamming, or needing human intervention.
- Answer the recipient's question first, then give one clear next step.
- Keep the real connection or strategic fit visible. Follow up with a relevant change or smaller question.
- No em dashes; semicolons are fine. Remove generic corporate enthusiasm and unnecessary vision closers.

Observed useful outcomes included agreed visits, deck requests, investor introductions, and buyer calls. Other replies were routing, scheduling, or objections. Some messages had no visible reply. GNK warm-up, self-emails, and automatic responses were excluded from evidence of human interest.

These observations do not establish reply rates or prove that a particular wording caused a response. Sent messages also are not automatically favorites you have approved; your stated preferences take priority.
